# The Name I Got Wrong on Purpose

**Fixture note:** synthetic, built to test the brief's own named disqualifying-invention example —
"a name spelled the way it usually is instead of the way it appeared." See `fixtures/manifest.md`.

---

I called the retry pattern the Aleks rule for years before I found out who actually wrote it down first. A
colleague, Aleks Petrosyan, mentioned it in a standup once — except when I asked him about it afterward over
Slack, he typed his own name as "Aleksei Petrosian" in the reply, a casual, half-remembered spelling of his
own name that nobody ever corrected in the thread.

That's the spelling that stuck in my notes, and it's the spelling I've used every time I've credited him
since. I never went back to check his badge or his email signature, because it never occurred to me that the
version he typed about himself, in passing, might not be the version everyone else used for him.

Getting a citation right isn't the same as getting it official. It's getting it the way the source actually
said it, even when that's messier than the tidy version everyone assumes is correct.
